01 - Assignment

I redesigned the Macalester MSCS department site so a first-year can tell the four majors apart, and a current student can reach courses, without reading the homepage as a bulletin. Scope was information architecture, first-screen hierarchy, and the path into Majors and Courses. I owned the UX in Figma for Macalester MSCS.

Identify

Name the barriers students hit when they try to tell the four majors apart or find a course.

Design

Structure the site around program, majors, courses, and people instead of a single long scroll.

Outcome

Let a student leave the first visit knowing what MSCS is and which door to open next.

02 - Uncovering the Problem

On the live site, a visitor lands on a program statement, then scrolls through diversity, internships, alumni lists, and news. The four majors wait behind one sidebar label. The decision that mattered was where a student chooses a major, not how much department content could fit on one page.

03 - Prioritizing Findings

Three frictions decided the first screen. Everything else could wait.

Four majors, one door

Mathematics, statistics, computer science, and data science share a label. A student cannot scan the difference until they leave the homepage.

News sits above the program

Happenings and alumni lists push major choice below the fold for anyone who arrived to pick a path.

Courses are a second hunt

Course Descriptions live in the sidebar, not next to the major that needs them.

How might we help a student understand MSCS, pick a major, and reach courses without reading the department as a bulletin?

04 - Research to Design

The map follows the job. Start names the combined department. Four major doors sit next. Courses and people come after a path is chosen. News moves to After Mac, where it supports the story instead of interrupting it.

05 - Learning and Impact

A combined department still needs separate doors

MSCS is stronger because the fields sit together. The site is weaker when that fact hides the four majors. Integration belongs in the story. Choice belongs in the navigation.

What I would do next

Prototype the four doors in Figma and compare click paths to Majors against the live homepage. Keep news, but not on the first screen.

Liberal arts identity has to be scannable

The live site already says students do not give up other parts of themselves. That line only works if a visitor can find it in one screen, then act.
