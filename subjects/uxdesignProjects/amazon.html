Offline Mode — Creating Offline Experience for Amazon's Warehouse Associates

Utilized competitive review and research data to improve offline mode for Amazon's warehouse associates.

Timeline
May – August 2026
Team
Workforce Solutions Transformative Tech
Role
UX Designer Intern
Skills
Figma, FigJam, Slack, Chime

01 - Assignment

This assignment came from a software engineering intern on the team who was already building an offline mode for warehouse associates. I was tasked with creating the offline experience and the interface that would carry it.

With a technical background, I took on how mobile apps cache information when Wi-Fi is gone, and how much of that information stays once the connection is fully cut. The problem we needed to solve was how to design an interface that tells users the app is disconnected from the information it can fetch.

Understand

Learn how the app caches and retains information when there is no Wi-Fi.

Interface

Design an offline experience associates can still use in the warehouse.

Inform

Make disconnection visible when the app cannot fetch information.

02 - UNDERSTANDING THE PROBLEM

When Wi-Fi drops, the app loses its path to the server. Requests already in flight fail or time out. Live records, updates, and anything that lives upstream can no longer be fetched.

What remains is whatever the app already stored on the device. Apps do not keep everything. They keep the slice most likely to let someone finish the job they opened the app for. That usually includes the last screen, identity, today’s tasks, and recently viewed records. Live or rarely used information is withheld until the connection returns. The interface has to make that split visible. It should show what is still here, and it should show that the app can no longer fetch.

Disconnect detected
Cache takes over
Priority data stays

I reviewed Workday and Google Maps to see how a work app and a best-in-class consumer app each handle that split. Those two competitors gave me the context and the communication pattern for an offline experience aligned with this app’s objective. The experience should tell associates they are disconnected, and it should still surface the information the app actually retained.

Workday

Workday is a workforce app, close to the tool associates already use at work. I chose it to see how an enterprise product keeps job-critical information available when the network is gone, instead of failing into a blank screen.

Google Maps

Maps is the offline pattern people already recognize. I chose it because it names the outage and still lets you complete the core job from cache. That is the status model this experience needed. It should not hide the disconnect, and it should keep the retained information usable.

03 - Research to Design

Competitive review left two directions. One keeps a usable shell and tries to hold static information when the network is gone. The other stops the experience and asks for Wi-Fi before anything live is shown.

I chose the first pattern. The app demands Wi-Fi before it presents the information. An internal enterprise tool is not a map you can keep in a download. Associates open A to Z for records that change in the moment. A time card, a shift, a pay figure, or a message is only useful if it is the current one from the server.

Enterprise tools lean on Wi-Fi because the source of truth is upstream. Caching yesterday’s schedule or last week’s pay would look like a working app and still be wrong. That is more dangerous than an empty screen. Workday and Google Maps already use a version of this restraint. They do not disclose the information that belongs to a live fetch until a connection can retrieve it for that moment. Maps will not invent live traffic without a network. Workday will not invent a workforce record the server has not returned.

That pattern belongs on Amazon’s A to Z app. The associate should know the app is disconnected. They should not be asked to act on rows that failed to fetch. Demanding Wi-Fi before presenting the information keeps the offline mode honest about what the enterprise tool can actually promise.

04 - Learning and Impact

An empty offline screen can be the honest one

Keeping a populated shell without a network looks finished and still misleads. Asking for Wi-Fi before live records appear taught me that restraint is part of the design, not a missing screen.

Offline work has to be designed with engineering

Caching, failed HTTPS requests, and what the device can keep are engineering facts. Pairing with the intern on the team made those facts the brief, so the interface described a real disconnect instead of a guessed one.

Competitive patterns only transfer when the risk matches

Workday and Google Maps withhold live information until a connection can fetch it for that moment. On A to Z that same rule protects associates from acting on stale enterprise data, which is why the empty Wi-Fi gate was the pattern I kept.
