Quick Glance

01 - Initial Research

I was tasked with improving the information architecture of the Talent & Equity Resources page using existing VoC data and usability tests on the Human Resources experience.

City of Saint Paul needed a compact, efficient way to organize a large set of subpages so people could find what they need.

How might we restructure a large, complex set of resources so employees

can navigate intuitively and find what they need?

02 - Planning

City of Saint Paul needed a strategy for organizing a significant number of subpages into a neatly compacted and very efficient structure to create an improved user experience. The structuring method needed to fulfill three criteria:

Users-In-The-Loop

Keeping the structure grounded in how users expect to navigate it.

Labels

Identify which resources users expect to find together.

Hierarchy

Translate those patterns into intuitive categories and navigation.

Taxonomy Development and Sitemap Creation didn’t give me a mental model of the user’s and it strongly relied on discovery data. The initial research were based on help requests and surveys that didn’t guide us to the solution with information discovery.

Instead, I did an Open Card Sorting Activity in order to establish users as the backbone of our findings, utilize relevant labels that align with our user’s mental model, and establish a hierarchy of the web interface.

03 - Execution

Category Title
Subpage Titles

Open Card Sorting Activity consisted of sticky notes
and empty sections

Instructions:

  • Group cards in a way that feels natural to you.
  • Feel free to change your groupings as you go.
  • You may leave cards uncategorized if you’re unsure where they belong.
  • Think aloud and share any questions or feedback throughout the activity.

I translated the existing subpages into individual cards and asked participants to organize them into groups that made sense to them. Because the activity was open, participants created and labeled their own categories rather than working within the site’s existing structure.

04 - Synthesis

The mindset I established when doing the card sorting activity was understanding the gap between what users were saying and what the users were doing.

Participants found strong associations between the titles of each page which created ambiguity on the purpose behind each.

Throughout each interview, I noticed a misalignment between the title of the subpages and what the pages really contained. Participants presented confusion on the titles and differentiating between page names.

Joining the Talent Community Working in Saint Paul

Working In Saint Paul ≠ Working In City of Saint Paul

I found that participants would abandon cards like “Applicant Data Practices”, “Senior Innovation Consultant” and “City of Saint Paul Insurance Requirement” due to complete lack of understanding.

“This group seemed random and I don’t know how they’re very unrelated”

Participant #10

I constructed the Information Architecture from a bottom-up approach. Starting with the lower levels and using participant patterns to work up towards the major categories. This is done in order to resolve lower level content first.

This would involve renaming pages. A good example would renaming “City of Saint Paul Insurance Requirements” to “Certificates of Insurance & Plan Description”.

06 - Learning and Impact

Card titles are not self-explanatory to users

Going into this project, I assumed the existing subpage titles were reasonably clear since they’d been live for years. The open card sort proved otherwise and surfaced the pattern of confusion.

Users organize by surface-level pattern-matching (keywords)

As the Designer, I learned to correct for surface-level patterns and to create an IA that is user-validated and not just encoding a naming coincidence.

Bottom-up IA construction surfaces exactly where content needs fixing

Building the hierarchy bottom-up meant that when a card failed to form a stable cluster at any tier, I could pinpoint WHY. That diagnostic clarity wouldn’t have emerged from a top-down approach.
