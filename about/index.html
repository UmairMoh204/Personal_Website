About

Hello! My name is Hamza Mohammed, a UX Designer and Researcher who specializes in enterprise/internal technology. I graduated from Macalester College with a Bachelor’s Degree in Computer Science and Cognitive Science with a Psychology minor. At Macalester, I developed technical skills in Python, Java, C, JavaScript, R Language, and SQL. At Macalester, I developed the skills to write code and analyze the strengths and weaknesses of coding languages. My journey into UX Design began when I asked myself a question for a project I was building in my Computer Science class:

"Why does this Checker's game look and feel very off?"

This would motivate my curiosity in UX Design that lead to my decision to enroll at Prime Digital Academy, an immersion training program preparing individuals for careers in software engineering and UX. This would lead to amazing opportunities to work on projects where I create real user experiences at the City of Saint Paul and Amazon. I’m always exploring new ways to bridge design and technology to create experiences that are not only functional but genuinely meaningful.

UX journey

01 · Macalester College (As a Posse Scholar)

As a first-generation Posse Scholar I learned to find my footing in college and to voice my position in global affairs. Macalester asked more of me than becoming a computer scientist. I learned to advocate for change and human rights, and to stay in open dialogue on contemporary issues.

02 · Prime Digital Academy

Prime introduced me to UX principles and methods, and taught me the full process from research through design. That sequence laid the foundation for how I navigate UX. I learned to hold the work from both a user standpoint and a business standpoint.

03 · Human-Computer Interaction · Macalester

HCI let me apply Computer Science and Cognitive Science to technical constraints, and still use UX methods on projects. I learned 3D and VR process, and how to make 3D prototypes that solve a problem. The work taught me to think critically and stay with a problem until it yielded.

04 · City of Saint Paul

Saint Paul was my first role as a user researcher. I learned civic tools, how to work a site in a refresh, and how to finish the first phase of a UX process with a clean handoff. Growing up here made it personal, because I already knew why people need the resources available to them.

05 · Amazon

At Amazon I designed inside a real org, using a functional design system with real constraints. It was my first time designing internal experiences for associates. I learned the importance of progressive disclosure, and of understanding ingress at every stage of a UX process.

Hamza Mohammed 01 · Posse Foundation

Becoming a Posse Scholar

I came to Macalester as a Posse Foundation Leadership Scholar — one of ten students from my city chosen to go to college as a cohort, not as a solo applicant.

Posse looks for public high school students with extraordinary leadership potential who traditional admissions might miss. Scholars receive a full-tuition leadership scholarship. Before campus, the posse trains together for eight months. On campus, you keep meeting as ten — with a mentor — for all four years.

That is the part that changed how I work. The scholarship was the door. The posse was the practice: sitting in a circle, telling the truth, holding the room when it got uncomfortable, and being responsible for nine other people, not only for my own grades.

The photos here are from that life — Posse Plus Retreat at Camp St. Croix, conversations about truth and belonging, meetings back on campus, and the larger Macalester community we were being asked to lead inside of.

Hamza Mohammed 02 · Prime Digital Academy

Becoming a UX designer

I joined Prime Digital Academy because Computer Science was teaching me how to build systems — not how to sit with the person who has to use them.

As a CS student I could write the feature. I could not yet defend why that feature should exist, in that order, for that person. Cognitive Science was already pulling me toward how people think. UX was the missing half of the work: research, flows, critique, and a product someone else could actually finish.

Prime was a full-ride UX certification and a room that treated design as practice, not as a side interest. We mapped what was important on the wall, drew the screens by hand, walked the architecture of a site, and shipped with a cohort. That is why I went into UX from CS — so the thing I build has a person on the other side of it, not only a passing test.

Hamza Mohammed 03 · Human-Computer Interaction · Macalester

HCI — putting people next to the system

HCI at Macalester is where I took what Prime had made a craft and put it next to Computer Science and Cognitive Science — in a room where most of my classmates were meeting UX for the first time.

Prime had already taught me the sequence: research, flows, critique, ship. In HCI I learned the why underneath that sequence — how a person actually holds a system, not only how a screen is drawn. CS gave me the build. CogSci gave me the mind. The course asked for both on the same desk.

Because I had studio hours behind me, I could translate. Stickies, paper wireframes, information architecture, a critique that names what is working — those were not new to me, and they were new to a lot of the room. I used that to pull the work forward without making the class about me: share the method, then get out of the way so someone else could run the next pass.

We built in public. A Mac Daily prototype. A VR piece that put a body in space instead of a page. Remote standups when the team could not share a table. The learning was not that I already knew UX. It was that Prime had given me a language I could hand to people who were just arriving — and HCI gave that language a research spine.
